1Anatomy of an AgentContext5 min

A Cortex Agent is a planner (an LLM) plus a set of tools it can call, plus instructions that tell it how to behave. Engineers build every part, but most parts are only as good as the business knowledge that went into them.

Engineer builds
Semantic View
The agent's business dictionary. You supply: metric definitions, synonyms ("PR" = requisition), which field is authoritative, fiscal calendar.
Engineer builds
Cortex Search
Finds answers in documents. You supply: which documents are the source of truth, and which are stale or superseded.
Engineer builds
Custom tools
Deterministic checks and actions. You supply: the rules as a decision table: condition, outcome, owner.
Engineer builds
Instructions
How the agent plans and answers. You supply: who uses it, what a good answer looks like, what it must never do.
Engineer builds
Multi-agent routing
Which specialist handles what. You supply: domain boundaries and who owns each domain.
Engineer builds
Evaluation
How we know it works. You supply: golden questions with the expected answer and how to verify it.

The three questions
Q1. What was our requisition escalation rate in Q3 2026, and what were the main reasons for escalation?
Q2. Should PR-260017 be escalated?
Q3. What does "fully executed" mean in our review policy, and does a pending addendum count toward authorized value?
Reveal: what the two agents said in our test run

Agent wording varies run to run. The pattern below is what to look for.

Q1: escalation rate

Hedged

Basic

"I can't answer this from the available data. There's no concept of requisition escalation in it." It then asks you to define escalation.

Correct

Well-specified

"44.1%: 26 of the 59 in-scope requisitions (≥ $100K)." Leading reason: contract not fully executed (14). Routing: Tier 1 to the Director of Procurement.

Q2: a single PR

Wrong, confidently

Basic

"From a contract-compliance standpoint, PR-260017 is clean and does not need escalation... the vendor on the requisition matches the vendor on the contract." It then invents an aging rule (141 days old) that isn't in any policy.

Correct

Well-specified

"Yes, escalate." Vendor match FAIL: the PR names Redwood Office Supply, but contract C-1003 belongs to a different supplier. Cites Policy §3 and routes to the Procurement queue.

Q3: a definition

Can't answer

Basic

It has no documents. At best it shows the status column's values and guesses.

Correct

Well-specified

Signed by both parties; "Pending Signature" is not executed. Pending addendums do not count; authorized value = MSA + executed addendums only. Cites Policy §3.

The lesson: "I don't know" is the good failure. The dangerous one is Q2: a fluent, confident, wrong answer, produced because nobody told the agent which vendor field is authoritative or what the escalation rules are. Every gap you found maps to a line missing from the one-line request.
Discuss (2 min): For each wrong or hedged answer, which of the six anatomy parts was missing? (Q1: a definition and a metric. Q2: an authoritative field and a decision rule. Q3: a source document.)

4Multi-Agent and CoWorkCoWorkMulti-Agent6 min

Real Finance questions cross domains. Instead of one giant agent, build specialists (each owned by the team that knows the domain) and a coordinator that reuses them. In CoWork, choose Finance Assistant (Multi-Agent) and ask:

Give me a Q3 2026 Finance risk summary: which Tier 1 vendors had procurement escalations, and did any entity have unusual cash movements?

Watch the reasoning panel: it calls the Procurement tools and the Treasury tools, then combines them. Expected: 3 Tier 1 vendors with escalations (Ironbridge Security 8, Harbor Payments Tech 6, Meridian Data Systems 2), plus 3 cash outliers in Q3 (Bank Operating Jul 31, Broker-Dealer Aug 19, Tech Platform Sep 8).

Why this matters for your request. In our first build, this exact question returned the wrong Tier 1 totals. The coordinator looked up vendor tier through the contract's vendor, so every vendor-mismatch PR was credited to the wrong supplier. The fix was one business sentence: "For any vendor or tier breakdown, use the supplier on the requisition." Nobody on the engineering side could have known that without asking.

Try a follow-up, then make it yours

  • Chart it: "Chart escalated dollars by vendor tier by month."
  • Save & share: save the answer as an Artifact and share it with a colleague.
  • Automate it: ask CoWork to deliver the Finance risk summary every Monday at 8am as an Automation.
  • Give feedback: use thumbs up/down with a comment. That feedback is how engineers find the next gap after launch.

Forecasting & anomaly detection: a preview

Ask the Treasury Liquidity Monitor: "Were there any unusual daily cash movements since April 2026?" It finds the four seeded outliers using a simple rolling statistical screen (3+ standard deviations from the trailing 30-day mean). It's a screen, not a forecast.

For cash-flow forecasting or augmenting TM1 driver-based forecasts, engineers would add a Snowflake ML forecasting or anomaly-detection model as another tool on this same agent. Your request stays the same shape: what to predict, at what grain, over what horizon, what counts as unusual, and who acts on it. A dedicated ML workshop will go deeper.
5Exercise 2: Fix the Vague RequestExercise7 min

This is the request that produced the Basic agent:

"Build an agent that checks POs against contracts."

In pairs (or solo), rewrite it by answering the six prompts below in one or two lines each. Use what you saw in Steps 2 to 4.

  1. Who and why: who uses it, in which decision, how often?
  2. Definitions: which terms need a precise meaning? (e.g. "in scope," "executed," "escalation rate")
  3. Rules: write the decision as if → then → owner.
  4. Sources of truth: which systems and documents are authoritative? Which field wins when two disagree?
  5. Guardrails: what must it never do or say? Who can see what?
  6. Golden questions: three questions with the exact answer you'd expect.
Reveal: the request that produced the Procurement Review Assistant

Who and why: Procurement reviewers and Strategic Finance, daily, deciding whether a Coupa PR/change order ≥ $100K can be auto-cleared or needs full review. Goal: cut manual review of ~6,000 approvals a year while lowering control exposure.

Definitions: In scope = amount ≥ $100,000. Executed = signed by both parties; Pending Signature is not executed. Authorized value = MSA + executed addendums only. Escalation rate = escalations ÷ in-scope PRs.

Rules: Auto-clear only if all five pass: contract reference present; PR supplier = contract supplier; contract executed; PR date within contract term; cumulative committed ≤ authorized value. Any failure → Escalate. Tier 1 → Director of Procurement; others → Procurement queue. Change orders count toward cumulative spend.

Sources of truth: Coupa for requisitions, Ironclad for contracts, Procurement Review Policy for definitions. For vendor breakdowns, use the supplier on the requisition.

Guardrails: Read-only; never approve, reject, or edit. Always cite the policy section. State the time-period assumption when a question is ambiguous.

Golden questions: Q3 2026 escalation rate → 44.1% (26/59). PR-260017 → Escalate, vendor mismatch. "Does a pending addendum count toward authorized value?" → No, Policy §3.

Poll: Your facilitator will show three sample requests. For each, vote: Could an engineer build this without scheduling a meeting?

Codify and Share Your Knowledge

The best Finance-to-engineering handoffs treat business knowledge as a versioned artifact that maps directly onto what engineers build, not as a meeting. Six artifacts cover almost everything:

Your knowledgeWrite it asEngineer turns it into
What terms and metrics meanBusiness glossary: term, definition, formula, owner, source tableSemantic View descriptions, synonyms, metrics
What the right answer isGolden questions: question, expected answer, how to verifyVerified queries + agent evaluation set
How decisions get madeDecision table: condition → outcome → owner, plus exceptionsCustom tools, views, instructions
Which documents to trustSource register: document, owner, refresh cadence, what's supersededCortex Search service + filters
Who may see and do whatAccess matrix: persona × data × allowed actionsRoles, masking and row access policies
What good looks likeResponse guide + acceptance criteria: sample answer, must-never list, pass thresholdResponse instructions; evaluation before publishing

Working agreements that make it stick

  • One brief per agent, one named business owner. Keep it where engineers work (a shared doc or repo), versioned, not buried in email.
  • Start narrow. One decision and ~10 golden questions that pass beats "a Finance agent."
  • Definitions follow the data. Confirm definitions against real rows. That's where the "which vendor field?" surprises show up.
  • Golden questions are the contract. The agent ships when an agreed share of them pass, and they're re-run after every change.
  • Launch is the start. CoWork feedback and new questions flow back into the brief; the business owner reviews them monthly.
  • Say what it must not do. Read-only, no approvals, who can see PII: guardrails belong in the request, not left for the build.
